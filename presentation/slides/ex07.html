New here: the ring buffer

The kernel half reserves and submits one record per lookup; Go receives it on a channel. The middle row of the previous slide.

examples/07-dns-latency-uprobe/ · dnslat.bt · dnslat.bpf.c + main.go

uprobes do for user space what kprobes do for the kernel. We hook getaddrinfo in libc, so every program that resolves names through glibc shows up with its lookup latency, a classic hidden cause of slow requests. This is also the first example that streams events instead of aggregating: the kernel half reserves a record in the ring buffer and submits it, and the Go half receives each record on a channel and decodes it. That is the middle row of the diagram on the previous slide. libbpf attaches automatically from the section name and finds libc.so.6 in /usr/lib64 on Rocky.
