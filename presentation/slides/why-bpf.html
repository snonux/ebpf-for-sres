Why not iostat, vmstat, top?

Counters say that it is slow. BPF says who, what and why.

Averages hide the tail

A 2 ms average await can be 99% fast reads and a few 200 ms stalls. BPF gives the full histogram.

Context, not counters

Each event carries the process, thread, file, arguments and latency, so you can join it to the code.

Still use them first

They are cheap and everywhere. Reach for BPF when they say that something is wrong but not where.

Before strace and perf, most of us start with iostat, vmstat and top, and we should: they are cheap and installed everywhere. But they read counters from /proc and print averages per interval for the whole box or the whole device. They tell you that the disk is slow or the run queue is long, not which process, which file, or which request, and an average hides exactly the outliers that cause timeouts. BPF tools answer the follow-up question at the event level, with distributions instead of averages, which is why each row has a BPF counterpart, several of them in today's examples.
